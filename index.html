UCSC Schedule Exporter

Your UC Santa Cruz class schedule → Google Calendar, in one click.

UCSC Schedule Exporter is a free Chrome extension for UC Santa Cruz students. It reads the class schedule shown on the two official UCSC scheduling pages and turns it into calendar events — either added directly to your Google Calendar, or saved as a standard .ics file you import yourself into Apple Calendar or Google Calendar.

What it does

Open your schedule on MyUCSC (my.ucsc.edu → Enrollment → Class Schedule, List View) or on MyScheduler (ucsc.collegescheduler.com, the “Potential Schedule” page), then click the extension icon. The popup:

  • lists every class it found — course, section, meeting days and times, and room — each with a checkbox so you choose exactly what to export;
  • One-click Google Calendar: after you sign in, creates one recurring weekly event per class (running until the last day of instruction, skipping campus holidays), on a calendar you pick or a new one it makes;
  • Download .ics: builds a calendar file entirely in your browser and downloads it — no sign-in, nothing sent anywhere;
  • adds a reminder you choose (none / 10 / 15 / 30 / 60 minutes, or a custom value) to every event;
  • optionally adds a final-exam event for each class, placed from the UCSC Registrar’s final examination schedule;
  • offers Undo last export, which removes exactly the events the last run created.

Why it asks for Google Calendar access

Google access is used only if you choose the one-click option. When you do, the extension requests these scopes:

Scope Purpose
calendar.events Create the class and final-exam events you selected (and delete them if you click Undo).
calendar.calendarlist.readonly Show the list of calendars you can write to, so you can pick a destination.
calendar.app.created Create a new calendar if you choose the “＋ New calendar” option.

The extension only creates and deletes events it made itself. It never reads, changes, or deletes your existing calendar events. All schedule parsing and .ics generation happen locally in your browser. Nothing is ever sent to the developer — there is no server, no account, no analytics, and no tracking. Full details are in the privacy policy.
